# Subtopic and trap codes (nurzycom progress index v1)

Every question gets exactly ONE `primary` subtopic code, up to TWO `secondary` codes (only if the question really tests them too),
any `flags` that apply, and a trap code for EVERY wrong option (every option except the key). Use only the codes below.

## Reading and Writing subtopics
Standard English Conventions — Boundaries:
- b_splice — joining two independent clauses: comma splice / run-on (choice between "," "." ";" etc. where one option fuses two sentences)
- b_fanboys — comma + coordinating conjunction (and, but, so, yet, or, for, nor) between clauses
- b_semicolon — semicolon use (between clauses with/without a conjunctive adverb, or a list whose items contain commas)
- b_colon_dash — colon or single dash introducing an explanation, list, or elaboration
- b_nonessential — paired punctuation around a nonessential element (two commas / two dashes / parentheses), appositives
- b_no_break — NO punctuation allowed: between subject and verb, verb and object, before a restrictive clause, inside a name/title
- b_intro — punctuation after an introductory word/phrase/clause
- b_other — quotation, possessive-list or other punctuation not covered above
Standard English Conventions — Form, Structure, and Sense:
- f_sv_agree — subject–verb agreement; finding the main subject and verb (often with a long modifier in between)
- f_pronoun — pronoun–antecedent agreement or pronoun case
- f_tense — verb tense / aspect consistency with the context
- f_verb_form — finite vs non-finite verb forms (participle, infinitive, gerund): does the sentence have a main verb
- f_modifier — dangling or misplaced modifier (who/what the opening phrase describes)
- f_possessive — possessive vs plural, apostrophes (its/it's, their/they're, -'s vs -s')
- f_noun_number — singular/plural noun agreement, other form questions
Expression of Ideas:
- t_contrast — transition of contrast (however, nevertheless, in contrast)
- t_concession — concession (admittedly, granted, still, even so)
- t_cause — cause/effect, consequence (therefore, as a result, thus)
- t_addition — addition, example, similarity (moreover, for example, similarly)
- t_sequence — time or sequence (subsequently, finally, meanwhile)
- t_restate — restatement, emphasis, summary, conclusion (in other words, indeed, in short, ultimately)
- rs_goal — rhetorical synthesis: pick the sentence that uses the notes to meet a stated goal
Craft and Structure:
- wic_precise — the most logical and precise word/phrase for a blank
- wic_meaning — "as used in the text, what does the word most nearly mean"
- ts_purpose — main purpose of the whole text
- ts_function — function of an underlined sentence/portion in the text
- ts_structure — overall structure of the text
- ctc — cross-text connections (how author of Text 2 would respond to Text 1, etc.)
Information and Ideas:
- ci_main — main idea of the text
- ci_detail — detail stated in the text ("according to the text", what is true of X)
- coe_text — textual evidence: which quotation/finding would support / weaken / illustrate a claim
- coe_quant — quantitative evidence: data from a table or graph that completes or supports a statement
- inf_complete — inference: which choice most logically completes the text

## Math subtopics
Algebra:
- alg_lin1 — linear equation in one variable (solve, number of solutions)
- alg_linfunc — linear function: slope/intercept meaning, writing y = mx + b, from a table or graph
- alg_lin2 — linear equation in two variables (standard form, points on the line, context)
- alg_system — system of two linear equations (solve, number of solutions, context)
- alg_ineq — linear inequality / system of inequalities
Advanced Math:
- adv_equiv — equivalent expressions (factoring, expanding, exponent rules, rational/radical expressions)
- adv_quad — quadratic: roots, vertex, factored/vertex form, discriminant, parabola graph
- adv_exp — exponential growth/decay, percent change per period
- adv_func — function notation, composition, transformations, polynomial graphs/zeros
- adv_nonlin — nonlinear equation or system (radical, rational, absolute value, line–parabola intersection)
Problem-Solving and Data Analysis:
- psda_ratio — ratios, rates, proportions, unit conversion
- psda_pct — percentages (percent of, percent change)
- psda_stats1 — one-variable data: mean, median, range, standard deviation, distributions, outliers
- psda_stats2 — two-variable data: scatterplots, line of best fit, model prediction
- psda_prob — probability, conditional probability, two-way tables
- psda_infer — inference from samples, margin of error, study design / causal claims
Geometry and Trigonometry:
- geo_areavol — area, perimeter, volume, surface area
- geo_lines — lines, angles, triangles (parallel lines, similar triangles, angle sums)
- geo_trig — right triangles, Pythagorean theorem, sin/cos/tan, special right triangles, radians
- geo_circle — circles: equation, arc length, sector, central/inscribed angles

## Flags (any number)
- archaic — archaic or old literary English in the passage
- literary — passage is fiction, poetry or drama
- two_texts — two passages
- data — table or graph in the question
- long — passage longer than about 120 words
- word_problem — math in a real-world context
- desmos — clearly faster with a graphing calculator
- spr — typed numeric answer (no options)

## Trap codes for wrong options (one per wrong option)
Reading and Writing:
- too_strong — too extreme / absolute / overstates the text
- off_topic — true or plausible, but does not answer the question asked (wrong focus, irrelevant detail)
- reversed — reverses the relationship, direction or the author's view
- half_right — partly right, partly wrong or unsupported
- scope — too broad or too narrow for the text
- unsupported — not supported by the text (outside knowledge, speculation)
- wrong_relation — wrong logical relation (transition logic, cause vs contrast)
- wrong_sense — common meaning of the word instead of the meaning in context; wrong connotation
- goal_miss — synthesis: uses true notes but does not meet the stated goal
- sounds_right — grammar/punctuation option that "sounds fine" but breaks the rule (fused clauses, wrong agreement…)
- wordy — redundant or unnecessarily wordy
Math:
- sign_error — sign or direction error
- step_short — stopped one step early (gives an intermediate value)
- wrong_quantity — answers a different quantity than asked (e.g. x instead of 2x, radius instead of diameter)
- operation — wrong operation or misapplied rule/formula
- misread — misread the graph/table/context or units
- other — a wrong option that fits none of the above

## Confidence
`high` — the code is clear from the question; `med` — two codes are close; `low` — unsure.
